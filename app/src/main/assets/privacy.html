Privacy Policy — ChopTorch

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Português (Brasil)

This policy applies to the production Google Play app ChopTorch: Shake Flashlight (package com.barbarioli1.choptorch), developed by Guilherme Barbarioli. Privacy and support contact: barbarioli.1+github@gmail.com.

Motion sensors and local processing

When you start gesture monitoring, ChopTorch reads accelerometer and gyroscope values to recognize the double-chop motion. It processes these readings on your device to calculate motion features and run the bundled neural-network model. This local sensor access is necessary for the feature; it is not data collection sent to a server.

The production build does not save raw motion readings or gesture examples. Sensor history used for detection is held temporarily in memory. Personal training, feedback capture, and model/example export are disabled in the production build.

Permissions and device features

  • Camera: used through Android's camera torch API to switch the flashlight on or off. ChopTorch does not capture photos or video.
  • Accelerometer and gyroscope: read while monitoring is active to detect the gesture. These sensor features do not require a runtime permission prompt on Android.
  • Foreground service and special-use foreground service: keep user-started gesture monitoring active while the app is not in the foreground, including when the display is locked.
  • Notifications: display the ongoing monitoring notification and its Stop action. Android 13 and later may ask for notification permission.
  • Wake lock: keep the CPU available while monitoring non-wake-up sensors. This can use additional battery.
  • Vibration: optionally confirm flashlight state changes. You can turn vibration feedback off in the app.

Monitoring starts only after you start it in the app and can be stopped from the persistent notification. Android, battery settings, or device-specific policies may still interrupt monitoring.

Information stored on your device

The production app stores your vibration-feedback preference in app-private storage. It includes a pretrained model in the app package; that model is not a record of your personal movements. The app can write operational timing and flashlight-state messages to the Android system log; it does not log raw sensor samples or transmit these messages.

The app disables Android backup and device-transfer backup for its private data. You can remove locally stored app data using Android Settings > Apps > ChopTorch > Storage > Clear storage, or by uninstalling the app. Android controls the lifecycle of temporary system logs and operating-system data.

Development builds distributed from the source repository may enable optional local training and sample export. Those development-only features are disabled in the production Google Play build.

Optional diagnostics

In internal closed-test builds that include diagnostics, sharing is ON by default on a fresh install. A previously saved opt-out remains OFF, and you can turn sharing off at any time using Share anonymous diagnostics in Settings. These builds use Firebase Authentication to create an anonymous account and Google Cloud Firestore to receive diagnostics. The app does not sign in or upload diagnostics while sharing is off.

When enabled, ChopTorch may upload a bounded summary of a movement attempt: the model confidence and derived 27-value feature vector, shape checks, prediction count and timing, whether the existing detector triggered, the requested flashlight state and its Android camera callback outcome, app version, device manufacturer/model, Android version, and screen/power-saving state. It may also upload a sensor-recovery or monitoring-start failure event. No raw accelerometer or gyroscope stream, camera image/video, location, contacts, advertising ID, Android ID, or hardware serial is collected by this feature.

Events are first stored in an app-private SQLite queue and uploaded in the background when a network is available. The queue is bounded to 500 events and 2 MiB. Turning sharing off stops future collection, cancels scheduled uploads, disables Firestore networking, and clears the local queue. A write already accepted by Google's SDK before you turn sharing off may still complete. Previously uploaded records are not removed by turning sharing off; request deletion by emailing barbarioli.1+github@gmail.com. Include the approximate dates and device/app details needed to locate your anonymous records. Data is retained until deleted following a request or manual project cleanup; automatic expiry is not configured.

Google processes the anonymous authentication and Firestore data as the app's cloud service provider under Google's terms and privacy policy. Firebase project owners can inspect and manually delete records. The app has no user-facing account or automatic server-side deletion feature.

Builds without the compile-time diagnostics option do not show the sharing control and do not collect or upload these diagnostics. No advertising or analytics SDK is used.

If you choose to contact support, your email app handles that action under its own privacy terms; ChopTorch itself does not make that email network request.

Children and changes

ChopTorch is a general-purpose utility and is not designed specifically for children. This policy may be updated if the app's data practices change. The latest version and its update date are published on the project website.
