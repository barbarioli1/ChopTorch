Privacy Policy — ChopTorch

Last updated: November 14, 2026

This policy applies to the Google Play app ChopTorch: Shake Flashlight (package com.barbarioli1.choptorch), including internal closed-test builds, developed by Guilherme Barbarioli. Privacy and support contact: barbarioli.1+github@gmail.com.

Motion sensors and local processing

When you start gesture monitoring, ChopTorch reads accelerometer and gyroscope values to recognize the double-chop motion. It processes these readings on your device to calculate motion features and run the bundled neural-network model. This local sensor access is necessary for the feature; it is not data collection sent to a server.

The production build does not save raw motion readings or gesture examples. Sensor history used for detection is held temporarily in memory. Personal training, feedback capture, and model/example export are disabled in the production build.

Permissions and device features

  • Camera: used through Android's camera torch API to switch the flashlight on or off. ChopTorch does not capture photos or video.
  • Accelerometer and gyroscope: read while monitoring is active to detect the gesture. These sensor features do not require a runtime permission prompt on Android.
  • Foreground service and special-use foreground service: keep user-started gesture monitoring active while the app is not in the foreground, including when the display is locked.
  • Notifications: display the ongoing monitoring notification and its Stop action. Android 13 and later may ask for notification permission.
  • Wake lock: keep the CPU available while monitoring non-wake-up sensors. This can use additional battery.
  • Vibration: optionally confirm flashlight state changes. You can turn vibration feedback off in the app.

Monitoring starts only after you start it in the app and can be stopped from the persistent notification. Android, battery settings, or device-specific policies may still interrupt monitoring.

Information stored on your device

The production app stores your vibration-feedback preference in app-private storage. It includes a pretrained model in the app package; that model is not a record of your personal movements. The app can write operational timing and flashlight-state messages to the Android system log; it does not log raw sensor samples or transmit these messages.

The app disables Android backup and device-transfer backup for its private data. You can remove locally stored app data using Android Settings > Apps > ChopTorch > Storage > Clear storage, or by uninstalling the app. Android controls the lifecycle of temporary system logs and operating-system data.

Development builds distributed from the source repository may enable optional local training and sample export. Those development-only features are disabled in the production Google Play build.

Data sent, sharing, and third parties

The standard production build does not include remote diagnostics or request the Internet permission. Internal closed-test builds may include diagnostics that use Firebase Authentication for an anonymous account and Google Cloud Firestore to receive events. In those builds, sharing is ON by default on a fresh install; a previously saved opt-out remains OFF. You can turn sharing off at any time in Settings.

When sharing is on, the app may send a bounded summary of movement attempts, including model confidence and derived movement features, detector and flashlight outcomes, event timing, app version, device manufacturer/model, Android version, and screen or power-saving state. Sensor-recovery and monitoring-start failures may also be sent. The feature does not send raw accelerometer or gyroscope streams, camera images or video, location, contacts, advertising ID, Android ID, or hardware serial number.

Events are queued locally and uploaded in the background. Turning sharing off stops future collection, cancels scheduled uploads, disables Firestore networking, and clears the local queue. A write already accepted by Google's SDK may still complete. Previously uploaded events are not deleted by opting out; request deletion by emailing barbarioli.1+github@gmail.com. Firebase project owners can inspect and manually delete records; automatic expiry is not configured.

The app does not use advertising or analytics SDKs. Google processes anonymous authentication and Firestore data as the app's cloud service provider under Google's terms and privacy policy.

If you choose to open the online policy or contact support, your browser or email app handles that action under its own privacy terms; ChopTorch itself does not make that network request.

Children and changes

ChopTorch is a general-purpose utility and is not designed specifically for children. This policy may be updated if the app's data practices change. The latest version and its update date are published on this page.
